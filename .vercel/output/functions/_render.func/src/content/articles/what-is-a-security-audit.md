A security audit is a systematic evaluation of an organization's security posture. It answers the question: "How well are we protecting our systems and data, and where are the gaps?"

## What gets audited

Auditors examine policies, procedures, configurations, and technical controls across:

- **Access controls** — Who has access to what? Is it appropriate? Is it reviewed regularly?
- **Network security** — Firewalls, segmentation, monitoring, remote access
- **Data protection** — Encryption, classification, retention, disposal
- **Incident response** — Is there a plan? Is it tested? Is the team prepared?
- **Change management** — Are changes documented, approved, and tested before deployment?
- **Vendor management** — Are third parties assessed for security risk?
- **Physical security** — Data centers, device disposal, clean desk policies
- **Compliance** — Are regulatory requirements being met?

## Types of audits

**Internal audits** — Conducted by your own team. More frequent, less formal, focused on continuous improvement.

**External audits** — Conducted by independent third parties. Required for certifications like SOC 2 and ISO 27001. Provide credibility with customers and partners.

**Compliance audits** — Focused on meeting specific regulatory requirements (HIPAA, PCI DSS, GDPR).

**Technical audits** — Deep-dive into specific technical areas: network architecture, application security, cloud configuration.

## The audit process

1. **Scope definition** — What's being audited and to what standard
2. **Documentation review** — Policies, procedures, architecture diagrams
3. **Interviews** — Talking to people who operate the systems to understand how things actually work vs. how documents say they work
4. **Technical testing** — Reviewing configurations, scanning for vulnerabilities, testing controls
5. **Findings and reporting** — Documenting gaps, risks, and recommendations
6. **Remediation** — The organization addresses findings
7. **Follow-up** — Verifying that remediation was effective

## The bottom line

Security audits provide accountability and visibility. They're uncomfortable — no one loves being examined — but they're how organizations identify blind spots before attackers do. A good audit doesn't just find problems; it drives meaningful improvement.
